1. Background and Objectives
1.1 Background
Bitcoin mining is fundamentally a large-scale, highly-parallel search over nonces and header
variations for SHA-256 double-hash (SHA-256d) solutions. While the cryptographic workload itself
is deterministic and brute-force, meaningful performance and profitability improvements are
routinely achieved via:
e Compute-path optimization (kernels, vectorization, reduced overhead, tighter loops)
e Scheduling and work distribution (lower coordination latency, fewer stale shares, improved
load balance)
e Energy efficiency and stability (undervolt/clock tuning, thermal control, adaptive policies)
e Reliability/uptime (fleet orchestration, failover, auto-remediation)
e Protocol/pool efficiency (Stratum performance, job negotiation efficiency, reduced rejects)
1.2 Objectives
VisionWave will design and deliver qSpeed-Mine™, a comprehensive mining acceleration and
control platform that:
e Maximizes effective hashrate at constant or improved stability.
e Improves Joules per TH (J/TH) via adaptive tuning policies and fleet-level optimization.

Appendix — qSpeed-Mine™ Bitcoin Mining Acceleration & 1000-Node Mining
Fabric
A1. Concrete 1000-Node Reference Topology
This reference topology is designed to support approximately 1000 mining nodes with high
availability, operational safety, and clear segmentation between management, mining traffic, and
telemetry. It is provided as an implementation-ready baseline and may be adapted to site
power/cooling constraints.
A1.1 Physical layout (racks, rows, failure domains)
e Node count: 1000 mining nodes (ASIC hosts or GPU rigs), grouped into failure domains by rack
and row.
e Example rack plan: 20 racks x 50 nodes (or 25 racks x 40 nodes).
e Row grouping: 4-5 racks per row, each row treated as an independent failure domain for safe
rollouts and throttling.
e Top-of-rack (ToR) switching: dual ToR per rack (A/B), each node dual-homed where practical;
otherwise A/B rack split.
e Power domains: circuit-aware grouping (PDU A/B) to ensure policy engine can cap load per
circuit and avoid breaker trips.
e Service zones: dedicate at least one ‘services rack' (or separate room) for
controllers/observability/storage with redundant power.
A1.2 Logical architecture (Controller HA, racks, segmentation)
Core logical components:
e qSpeed-Mine Controller Cluster (HA): policy engine, rollout manager, fleet scheduler,
configuration service.
e Message bus / work distribution: reliable low-latency channel for job routing and node
coordination.
e Metrics/logging pipeline: TSDB + log aggregation + dashboards; long-term cold storage
optional.
e Secrets & identity: centralized secrets store, mTLS identity for nodes, signed configuration
bundles.
e Node Agents: local miner control, telemetry collection, watchdog/auto-heal, safe-mode
enforcement.

A1.5 Rack group model
Define rack groups to enable staged rollouts and rapid containment:
Group size: 25-50 nodes per group (usually a single rack).
Rollout order: Canary group (1 rack) > Early adopters (3 racks) > Row rollout > Full fleet.
Automatic pause conditions: stale share spike, reject spike, temperature excursion, PSU/PDU
alarms, or network jitter above thresholds.
Fallback: one-click revert to last-known-good policy bundle; nodes enter safe-mode if controller
